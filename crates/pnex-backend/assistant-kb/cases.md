A **case** (Knowledge › Cases) reconstructs who, where, when, and with what evidence, for one question to resolve — an incident, a failure, a quality deviation. It holds dated **events**; objects (devices, media, persons, places, documents, pages, notes) are **attached** to events with a role. Nothing is copied: the case points at the organization's objects.

## What you can do
- **Cases** page: type a title and press Enter to open a case; filter by status (open, concluded, archived).
- **Layout**: a case opens full screen, like the flow editor. The top bar holds the status, the **Links / Map** switch (one large view at a time), *Frame*, the exports and **?**. The **+** button (top left) opens the objects panel. The inspector slides in from the right only when something is selected (Esc or *Close* hides it). The timeline sits at the bottom: *Fold* turns it into a thin playback bar (play, time, a strip with the events and the cursor), *Timeline* unfolds it.
- **Watch cases**: a flow with a *Check record* node opens one case per watched subject (for example one per partner, under one per certification) and keeps it up to date. The **Checks** panel above the timeline shows each check with its result (OK, unknown, finding), the rules not OK, its last run and since when it is unchanged. A run that changes nothing adds no event; a change closes the previous period and opens a new event. Archiving a watch case stops it: it is never reopened.
- **Timeline** (bottom of the workspace): click the *Events* line to place an instant, drag along it for a period; drag an event to move it. The cursor (click or drag the ruler) drives the map, the links graph and the inspector. **Play** replays the window at ×1, ×3 or ×8 real time, or *Fitted* (the whole window in 60 s); − / + / *Fit* zoom (*Fit* frames every event, even over decades). The two date fields set the window's start and end exactly. **Compact** (on by default) folds long stretches without events into narrow breaks labelled with their length (≈ 6 years), so events years apart stay readable; turn it off for a linear axis. When labels pile up, the rest gathers into a **+N** chip: click it to zoom onto those events. Over 31 days, actors' tracks and *Recompute* are off: zoom in. **?** (or the ? key) lists the keyboard shortcuts: Space play, ← → previous / next event, Shift + ← → one tick, + − zoom, 0 fit, N new event at the cursor, R retain the detection under the cursor, Esc close.
- **Attach**: drag an object from the **+** panel (*Objects* tab: the case members, or search the organization) onto an event, onto a member's lane (a new event with that member already attached), or onto *Undated*. In the inspector, *Attach an object…* does the same with the keyboard. The role is proposed by type — persons and organizations *Actor*, places and POIs *Context*, notes and hypotheses *Hypothesis*, flows *Source*, anything else *Evidence* — and changes from the inspector.
- **Undated**: the *Undated* tab of the **+** panel keeps the pieces not placed in time yet (its count shows on the tab); dragging one onto an event dates it.
- **Detach / remove**: ✕ next to a piece detaches it from the event (it stays in the case, undated). *Remove from the investigation* on an event takes the event out; its pieces stay in the case.
- **Inspector**: the **Detections · N** button of the timeline opens *The investigation at HH:MM* — events already happened and ongoing, where each tracked member is, the raw signals around the cursor with **Retain** to make one an event. On an event: title, start and end (UTC), instant or period, pieces with their role, analysis note. On an object: the events it is involved in, *Follow only its chronology*, its object page.
- **Signals**: hidden by default (the line then shows only the pending detections); the **Signals** button of the timeline shows them. Like changes close in time gather into one chip (*127 objects created*). They show what happened around the members — links opened or closed, object changes, ranges (shifts, maintenance windows), notifications of member flows. They are suggestions: retaining one creates an event; nothing is added by itself.
- **Detections**: when the case opens, and on **Recompute** (timeline toolbar), detectors read the tracks of the visible window and propose: two members *together* (within 40 m for at least 2 min), a member *stopped* (within 25 m for at least 15 min), a *signal lost* (no position for more than 5 min, or the track ending before the window). They show on the Signals line with a solid border and in the inspector under *Detections*: **Retain** makes an event (members attached as *Actor*, the place pinned on the map, the detector cited in the note), **Dismiss** keeps it aside for good — it is never proposed again. Detections also show on the **To validate** page (Knowledge menu) as *Case event*, with the case and the members: accepting one there retains it (*Correct and accept* lets you choose the event's title), rejecting dismisses it. A detection is a lead to verify, never a fact. These thresholds are the defaults: each case can set its own in the **Frame** drawer (*Detector thresholds*; empty = default); saving them recomputes, and detections already proposed stay as they were.
- **Map** (the *Map* view): every located member — a place with a location, an area's centre, a POI — has a marker; the **Located** list (top left) centres the map on one and opens it in the inspector, as a click on its marker does. Without a basemap provider the map says so (organization settings). Each tracked member's route is drawn dashed for the whole window, solid up to the cursor, stronger over the last tenth of the window; nothing is drawn across a signal gap. The selected event's place shows as a pin.
- **Import a track**: select a member (inspector), *Import a track (GPX, CSV)…*, pick the file. The preview gives the number of points, the span and the rows set aside (unreadable, coordinates out of range, duplicate time); when the file's times state no offset, give the time zone of the recording (the browser's is proposed) and *Read again*. **Import** keeps the points as case evidence with the file's SHA-256 fingerprint, frames the timeline on them and runs the detectors. Old recordings are accepted (no age limit, no telemetry retention). The same file onto the same member is imported once; ✕ removes an import. A CSV needs a time column (time, timestamp, date…), latitude and longitude, altitude optional; `,` `;` or tab separated.
- **Zones**: a *Place* (Investigation pack) gets a *Zone* — a circle (latitude, longitude, radius in metres) or a pasted GeoJSON polygon. A member zone is drawn on the case map, and the detectors propose *X in Y* for each visit of a tracked member (entry and exit instants interpolated; a visit cut by a signal gap shows as two).
- **Tracked members**: a device that publishes positions, or an object tracked by a device (*tracks* link, dated), gets a lane — covered spans in its colour, signal gaps hatched — and a marker on the map at the cursor's position. Click its name to follow only its chronology.
- **Open a case from an alert**: in Events or a notification channel's journal, expand a row and click **Open a case**: its first event is the alert at its instant, with the flow that raised it as *Source* and the flow's neighbours as *Context*.
- **Frame**: the question to resolve, the time window (computed from the events), the processing basis of personal data, the detector thresholds, the frozen states, the hypotheses board, the pages and, once archived, the purge.
- **Links** (graph panel): events are dark rings, objects discs coloured by family (people, organizations, places, pieces; claims, hypotheses and identifiers as coloured rings), sized by how many links they have; each attachment is a straight edge, ontology relations between members are curved — faded before they start, gone once closed at the cursor. Hovering or selecting a node fades everything outside its direct neighbours and shows their labels (edge labels when it has six links or fewer, or on hovering the edge). Objects linked to nothing stand in a column on the right until their links arrive. The toolbar switches between three views: **Network** (the free force layout), **Clusters** (one disc per object type, the most linked node at its centre, the type and its count written above; the lines show what ties the types together) and **Hierarchy** (layers along the links' direction: an owner or a funder above what it owns or funds, a group or a party above its members; objects without links in rows underneath). **Clicking a node** brings it to the centre of its orbit, in any view: its neighbours on a first ring, each with the link that ties it to the centre under its name, their own neighbours on a second ring, the rest hidden; the camera glides and frames it. Click another node to move on; **← Back** returns to the previous centre, then to the view. Picking a view in the toolbar leaves the orbit. What happens after the cursor fades. The same case always draws the same; a new node never moves the others, and a node dragged by hand stays where it is dropped and the arrangement is saved for everyone who opens the case (a viewer can move nodes for a better look, nothing is saved); *Re-layout* clears it and the automatic layout comes back. **Double-click an object** to see its neighbours counted by link and type, tick some and *Add to the graph*: they show with a double ring until **Undo expansion**, or **Keep** puts them in the undated tray. Up to 2 000 nodes are drawn (a parliament fits in one case); past 300 a *Laying out the graph…* status shows for about a second, and past 2 000 the rest is counted as hidden. When the browser cannot draw it (no WebGL), a *Simplified view* badge shows and the older drawing is used: events diamonds, relations dashed, the same gestures, at most 300 nodes, no *Hierarchy*.
- **Sub-cases**: a case can hold sub-cases, each a narrower reading of the same objects (links are shared, nothing is copied). **+ Sub-case** (strip above the view) opens one in a click, named *New sub-case* (rename it with the pencil); it takes the parent's legal basis. The strip shows the enclosing cases as a breadcrumb and the sub-cases with their member count; *Back* in the header goes to the parent case. In the parent's *Links* graph each sub-case is a node tied to the members it shares with the parent: click it to orbit it, **double-click** to open it.
- **Graph legend**: the chips of the *Links* view (People, Organizations, Places, Identifiers, Evidence, Other, with their count) hide or show a family. **Evidence** (pieces and claims) is hidden by default: each node they touch then shows a count pill after its label, so the graph stays readable; click the *Evidence* chip to draw them again.
- **How is it linked to…** (inspector of an object): pick another member of the case; the shortest chain of links between them (up to 4 links, through events too) is listed and everything else fades in the graph. *Clear the path* brings the graph back. Tick **Introduction path: links between people only** to follow only who knows, recommends, employs or directs whom, and group membership: the answer to "who can introduce me to this person?".
- **Payments**: an event becomes a payment when it has an **Amount** (inspector of the event: amount, three-letter currency code such as EUR, *Estimated* when only an order of magnitude is known) and attachments with the roles **Payer** and **Payee**. Several payers or payees share the amount evenly (shown as estimated). A payment without a payer or payee shows money from an *Unknown payer* or to an *Unknown payee*.
- **Money** view (next to *Story*): a Sankey diagram of the payments, payers on the left, payees on the right, band width = amount, one currency at a time (a selector shows when the case has several; amounts are never converted). When an intermediary received more than it paid out, the difference goes to **Untraced** — what is not documented yet. A band is dashed when one of its payments has no piece attached as evidence or source; pale when estimated; orange when the money goes back up the chain (a loop, often what you are looking for). Click a band for its payments (date, title, amount, *no piece*), a payment to open it; click a node to select the object. *Up to the cursor* keeps only the payments up to the timeline cursor.
- **Ownership** (inspector of a person or organization, with the Investigation pack): an *owns* link carries a **Share held** in percent. The inspector lists who holds the object (*Held by*) and what it holds (*Holds*), directly or *indirect*ly: shares multiply along a chain and add up over chains; a missing share shows *≥* or *?*. A person at 25 % or more is marked **Beneficial owner (computed)** — a deduction from the links entered, not a fact.
- **Track access log** (Frame): every read of a person's tracks — the timeline, the detectors, a freeze — is logged with who, when, which persons and which window (personal data). It is unavailable when telemetry is not configured.
- **Frozen states** (Frame): **Freeze state** (optional label) fixes the case as it is — events and their pieces with roles, undated pieces, board, pieces manifest, reviewed detections and the tracks around the events. Each state is numbered, carries a SHA-256 fingerprint, never changes and reads back identically even once the telemetry has expired; it saves as JSON (the fingerprinted bytes) or Markdown. Purging a person also removes the frozen states that name them. You cannot freeze a case for the user.
- **Hypotheses board** (with the Investigation pack): each hypothesis with the claims for and against it, then the claims without a source and those contradicted. No score: you conclude.
- **Pages** (with the Investigation pack for the templates): *5 whys*, *Ishikawa (6M)*, *Incident report*, *Investigation note*, *Presentation outline*, or blank: the page opens and joins the case as an undated piece. **Make it the conclusion** designates it; when the case is set to *Concluded*, that page is frozen in a named version *Conclusion*.
- **Purge at archiving**: once archived, *Personal data to purge* lists the persons and identifiers no other case and no outside link hold. Untick those to keep, then **Purge**: they are deleted for good.
- **Export**: downloads the case as one JSON file (`pnex.case/1`): events with their pieces and roles, undated pieces, hypotheses board, pieces manifest (source URL, SHA-256). A warning shows when it contains persons. **Markdown** (next to it) saves a readable version: frame, timeline with what each event involves, undated pieces, board, pieces. On the cases list, **Import** turns such a JSON file (or a frozen state) into a new open case of the same organisation; objects no longer there are listed, not re-created.
- From any device, media or site page, or any object page: **Add to a case** puts the object among the case's undated pieces.

## Good to know
- The ruler shows UTC; click the date next to the clock to switch to local time. Event start and end are entered in UTC.
- A person can be attached only once the case's processing basis is filled in (Frame): otherwise the gesture answers *case-legal-basis-required*.
- *track-import-timezone-required*: the file's times have no offset; fill in the time zone in the preview. *track-import-unreadable*: not a GPX, or a CSV without time, latitude and longitude columns. *track-import-empty*: no usable point (see the rows set aside).
- Importing a track onto a person needs the case's processing basis, as attaching one does.
- *case-proposal-stale*: the detection was already retained or dismissed (by you in another tab, or a colleague): reload the case.
- If someone saved an event while you were editing it, saving answers *ontology-version-conflict*: the workspace reloads, redo your change.
- No lane for a member: it has no position track in the window (or the positions are unavailable); its events still show.
- Signals and tracks follow the visible window; tracks are served for windows up to 31 days: zoom in to see them.
- A case is an ontology object: it also has an object page (properties, links, provenance).
- Ask the assistant about a case ("summarize what happened between 9:00 and 11:00", "which signals around the alert could be events?", "which claims have no source?"): it reads the workspace, the signals, the detections and the hypotheses board. It never attaches, detaches nor creates an event, and never changes a claim's status: it proposes, you act.
